#### **Executive Summary**

Shaw Environmental, Inc. (Shaw) conducted a soil investigation along the proposed State Route (SR) 132 alignment and right-of-way (R/W) in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California. This investigation was conducted at the request and authorization of Mr. Gary Gagliolo of the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) under Task Order No. 10-403500-BW, Contract No. 43A0078.

Caltrans is proposing to alleviate traffic congestion by constructing a four-lane expressway for SR 132 between SR 99 and Dakota Avenue in Modesto, California. According to Caltrans, a portion of a former FMC Corporation (FMC) property was purchased about 1961 for the expansion of SR 99. Three soil stockpiles are located within the proposed SR 132 alignment and R/W. Caltrans has told Shaw that these three stockpiles are believed to contain soil from the former FMC facility that processed barite ore or similar material. The stockpiles rise to average grades of approximately 3.5 to 7.5 meters (12 to 24.5 feet) above the surrounding grade.

Caltrans performs environmental investigations preceding engineering and construction projects to assess worker health and safety issues and to evaluate management issues including potential waste classification. Task Order 10-403500-BW requested analysis of antimony, arsenic, barium, chromium, iron, strontium, polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons, nitrate, and pH, and proposed the advancement of 50 soil borings within the stockpiles to facilitate the testing of soil samples in order to screen, predict, and advise for worker safety, and re-use and disposal of excavated soils. Unless refusal occurred, soil samples were taken from each boring at depths of approximately 0.15 meters (0.5 feet), 1.5 meters (5.0 feet), 3.0 meters (10 feet), 4.5 meters (15 feet), and 6.0 meters (20 feet). The investigation was divided into the following sections:

- Stockpile No. 1 (estimated volume of 19,100 m<sup>3</sup>) 15 borings
- Stockpile No. 2 (estimated volume of 73,370 m<sup>3</sup>) 25 borings
- Stockpile No. 3 (estimated volume of 13,230 m<sup>3</sup>) 10 borings

Based on this investigation, the primary contaminant of concern in the stockpiles is barium. Elevated concentrations of barium were reported in the stockpiles and the concentrations were compared to total threshold limit concentrations and soluble threshold limit concentration (STLC) values to classify the stockpiles. Shaw conducted a statistical evaluation of barium analytical data for each stockpile to aid in waste classification.

## 1.0 Introduction

This report has been prepared by Shaw Environmental, Inc. (Shaw) to present the results of the soil investigation conducted at the intersection of the proposed State Route (SR) 132 right-of-way (R/W) and SR 99 in Modesto, Stanislaus County, California (Figure 1). This investigation was conducted at the request and authorization of Mr. Gary Gagliolo of the California Department of Transportation (Caltrans) and in general accordance with Caltrans Contract Number 43A0078, Task Order Number 10-403500-BW (Caltrans, 2003).

Caltrans is proposing to alleviate traffic congestion by constructing a four-lane expressway for SR 132 between SR 99 and Dakota Avenue in Modesto, California. Three soil stockpiles are located within a commercial and residential neighborhood and within the proposed SR 132 alignment and R/W, and are believed to contain soil from a former FMC Corporation (FMC) facility that processed barite (a mineral, barium sulfate). An aerial photograph of the area is provided in Figure 2. According to Caltrans, a portion of the FMC property was purchased about 1961 for the expansion of SR 99. Portions of the soil from the former FMC facility may have been stockpiled within the proposed SR 132 alignment and R/W during the expansion of SR 99. The former FMC facility is under a Cleanup and Abatement Order with the Regional Water Quality Control Board (RWQCB) and a Voluntary Cleanup Agreement with the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC).

Caltrans performs environmental investigations preceding engineering and construction projects to assess worker health and safety issues and to evaluate management issues including potential waste classification. Task Order Number 10-403500-BW lists the potential contaminants of concern (COC) as: antimony, arsenic, barium, chromium, iron, strontium, polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), nitrate, and pH. Caltrans identified these COCs based on information provided by and discussions with the DTSC, the agency overseeing the remediation of the FMC facility.

#### 4.4.3 Barium

The primary COC identified during this investigation is barium. Elevated concentrations of barium were reported in soil.

Shaw conducted a statistical evaluation of barium analytical data for this project at the request of Caltrans. The statistical evaluation was conducted in general accordance with guidance provided in SW-8156, "Test Methods for Evaluating Solid Waste, Volume II: Field Manual, Physical/Chemical Methods" (EPA, 1986). The statistical evaluation was conducted to further

-N:lsacDP!2004|Prj\101\101715.4ac.doc 4-3

evaluate the concentration of barium within soil for stockpile characterization. The statistical evaluations addressed the following items:

- Calculation of arithmetic mean;
- Determination of normal or lognormal distribution of the sample data; and
- Calculation of the 90 percent Confidence Interval (CI) which provides a corresponding 95 percent Upper Confidence Level (UCL), interpreted as a 0.95 probability that the true mean for a given population is no higher than the calculated UCL.

The data from all sample intervals were combined into one data set for each soil stockpile. Histograms of the total metals results were constructed to evaluate the distribution of the total metals concentrations within the data sets (Appendix C).

The total barium data was found to be heavily skewed to lower concentrations and were not normally distributed. The data were lognormally transformed and the transformed data was also found not to have a normal distribution. Therefore, statistical analysis was conducted using non-para-metric techniques, which do not require that the data be drawn from a specific distribution.

The statistical analysis for the total barium data was conducted using the Bootstrap method to estimate the 95 percent UCL for the mean of the total barium data. Bootstrap methods are non-parametric techniques to infer the distribution of a statistic derived from a data set. Bootstrap methods construct a "distribution" for a statistic (in this case the mean) by resampling with replacement from the data set. A large number (B) of data subsets of size n (where n is the size of the data subset) are selected. The statistic is computed for each of the n data subsets of size n. This gives a sample of values of the statistic, rather than one value. Confidence limits for the population parameter that is estimated by the Bootstrapped statistic are then constructed using percentiles of the sampled distribution of the statistic.

An expected soluble barium concentration was obtained from regression analysis (model fit to the data) developed from the total/soluble barium data using Analytical Software's Statistix 7 program. A predicted soluble barium concentration for WET and TCLP data corresponding to the total barium 95 percent UCL was calculated for each stockpile, if applicable. ## 5.0 Conclusions and Recommendations

This section presents conclusions for all stockpiles.

- PAHs, nitrates, and soil pH do not appear to indicate a hazardous waste classification of the soils.
- The concentrations of antimony, chromium, iron, and strontium reported during this investigation would not result in excavated soil being classified as a hazardous waste.
- Arsenic was detected sporadically in samples from each stockpile. Arsenic was detected in only 4.5 percent of the total sample population.
- The primary contaminant of concern in the soil stockpiles is barium. Elevated
  concentrations of barium were reported in the stockpiles and the concentrations were
  compared to TTLC and STLC values to classify the stockpiles as hazardous or
  non-hazardous. A statistical analysis was performed to aid in stockpile
  characterization.
