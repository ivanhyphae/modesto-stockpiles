Thallium is not a contaminant of concern at this site, and the December 2023 exceedance does not indicate a release.** The per-location values for that event exist only in **Table 2** of the February 2024 and February 2026 storm water reports, which is excluded from the compiled source markdown (data tables are deliberately dropped project-wide because marker mis-rendered columns; see the `source-pipeline` learning). Read directly from the PDF and verified by character-offset alignment against the Thallium column header, December 19, 2023 was:
>
> | Location | Thallium | |
> |---|---|---|
> | SW-East | 18 µg/L | site |
> | SW-West | 15 µg/L | site |
> | **BG-West** | **17 µg/L** | **background** |
>
> The **background** location exceeded alongside the two site locations, against a 2.0 µg/L primary MCL and a **15 µg/L laboratory reporting limit** — all three results sit within ~20% of the detection floor. A stockpile release cannot elevate the background sample to the level of the site samples, so this is a single anomalous sampling day at a reporting limit poorly matched to its own standard, not a site signal. Thallium was non-detect in every subsequent event (Feb 2024, Feb 2025, Feb 2026) and DTSC approved each report with no thallium comment or follow-up requirement.
>
> The narrative prose in the source reports says only "except for thallium, which exceeded the primary MCL in storm water samples collected in December 2023," naming no locations or values. Thallium was tested throughout the Modesto Soil Stockpiles investigation as one of the seventeen Title 22 (CAM 17) metals and consistently behaved as a minor, largely non-detected constituent. Storm water sampling in December 2023 produced the site's only reported Primary MCL exceedance for thallium — an exceedance that also appeared at the background location and has not recurred (see correction above).

## Soil

Thallium was detected in stockpile soil samples at low concentrations in the Shaw 2006 SI (165 stockpile and 89 native soil samples from 51 borings, plus 24 background samples), a finding repeated consistently across the Final Feasibility Study (June 2014), the Draft Final and Final Remedial Action Plans, and the FEIR/EA documents. Unlike barium, lead, nickel, vanadium, and zinc — detected "considerably higher than background values" — and unlike arsenic, chromium, cobalt, and copper, which slightly exceeded background, thallium was grouped with beryllium, cadmium, mercury, and molybdenum as detected only "at low concentrations." It was never identified as a primary chemical of concern; DTSC named only barium, strontium, and lead as primary COPCs, with thallium among the "additional" COPCs tested. In the clean borrow-area characterization sampling for cap fill (Basin 5 and other borrow areas, multiple technical memos 2014–2020), thallium was not detected above laboratory reporting limits in any composite sample, supporting the conclusion that those borrow soils were suitable for use as clean cap cover.

## Groundwater

Across every groundwater monitoring round from May 2012 through April 2019 — spanning wells sampled under report series S9525, S9800, and S1200 — thallium was consistently not reported at concentrations equal to or greater than the method detection limit or practical quantitation limit. This pattern held through 2012–2013 quarterly rounds, the 2013–2016 monitoring events, and later rounds in 2017 and 2019. One anomaly is noted in the June 2012 report: CVRWQCB split samples analyzed by Excelchem Environmental Labs reported higher thallium concentrations in wells MW-2, MW-4, MW-5, and MW-6 than the site's own laboratory results, though at the same order of magnitude, and the report notes the 2006 and 2012 site-sample thallium results were internally consistent across that six-year gap despite different labs. Thallium has not been identified as exceeding any groundwater threshold at the site.

## Surface water and stormwater

Thallium was not reported above detection limits in any surface water or stormwater sampling round from the earliest events (2013 on-call sampling) through January 2018, including the October 2016, December 2016, March 2017, January 2018, and March 2018 rounds, and including both background (BG) and downgradient (PL) locations. The March 10, 2023 surface water sampling — the first event after road construction was completed and the stockpiles encapsulated — also did not report thallium above MDLs in background or surface water samples; the March 28, 2023 letter groups thallium with antimony, beryllium, cadmium, cobalt, nickel, and silver as not detected in that round.

This changed with the stormwater sampling conducted after Stantec identified two new stormwater sample locations and one new background location on November 2, 2022 (replacing the prior on-call locations made obsolete by SR 99/132 road construction). Both the 2024 and 2025 stormwater sampling reports state that reported TDS, sulfate, and metal concentrations from the March 2023, December 2023, and February 2024 events were below Primary and Secondary MCLs "except for thallium, which exceeded the primary MCL in storm water samples collected in December 2023." This is the only recorded Primary MCL exceedance for thallium anywhere in the site's monitoring history; the March 2023 event that preceded it did not show the exceedance, and neither did February 2024, February 2025, or February 2026. **The concentrations and the MCL are given in Table 2 of those reports — 18 / 15 / 17 µg/L at SW-East / SW-West / BG-West against a 2.0 µg/L MCL and a 15 µg/L reporting limit — but Table 2 is excluded from the compiled source markdown, so only the reports' prose is visible here. See the correction at the top of this article: the background location exceeded alongside the site locations, which rules out a site release.** No consequence of the December 2023 exceedance — further sampling requirement, agency response, or corrective action — is recorded, and DTSC's July 28, 2026 letter approves the February 2026 report as final with all metals within acceptable levels and no thallium comment.
