The Modesto Soil Stockpiles are three containment areas within the state right-of-way south of the SR-99/Kansas Avenue interchange in Modesto, holding approximately 160,000 cubic yards of soil containing metals — primarily barium, strontium, and lead — generated during construction of the SR 132 West Freeway/Expressway Project. Rather than removing the material, the project's remedy contains it in place behind retaining walls and bridge abutments and beneath the roadway, with clean fill cover over unpaved portions. DTSC approved the Remedial Action Plan (RAP) for the site on March 14, 2018, making the stockpiles a permanent on-site containment remedy rather than a removed-and-disposed waste stream, which is why long-term land use controls and monitoring attach to the property going forward.

## Regulatory approval and CEQA basis

Caltrans, as CEQA Lead Agency, prepared an Environmental Impact Report/Environmental Assessment (EIR/EA) and certified it on March 2, 2018. DTSC, acting as a CEQA Responsible Agency, relied on that certified EIR/EA for its own CEQA analysis of the RAP rather than preparing a separate environmental document. DTSC approved the RAP on March 14, 2018, and made findings under CEQA that the project would not have a significant effect on the environment, with mitigation measures made a condition of project approval. Caltrans adopted a Statement of Overriding Considerations for its own project, but DTSC's Notice of Determination specifies that this Statement does not apply to DTSC's approval — the two agencies' CEQA findings are procedurally linked (one document, one certification) but formally separate as to significance conclusions. The site is tracked in DTSC's EnviroStor database under two global ID numbers (60001626 and 50280024), reflecting the project's association with more than one EnviroStor site profile.

## Containment remedy and ongoing obligations

The approved remedy depends on physical containment — retaining walls, bridge abutments, roadway structure, and clean fill cover — to prevent direct exposure to the stockpiled metals-bearing soil and to limit infiltration of surface water into groundwater beneath the stockpiles. Because the remedy is engineered containment rather than removal, its protectiveness is conditioned on the long-term integrity of those physical features: the RAP calls for annual inspection of the pavement and other containment features, together with land use controls and annual groundwater monitoring, to confirm the remedy continues to function. This makes the stockpiles a standing inspection and monitoring obligation rather than a closed remediation.

## Thallium in stockpile soil and stormwater

Thallium was detected at low concentrations in the stockpile soil documented in the June 2014 Final FS Report and had not been detected in groundwater or in surface water at the site before 2023. Stormwater sampling in March 2023 did not report thallium above the California primary MCL. A stormwater sampling event in December 2023 produced the only reported exceedance of the thallium MCL at the site. **Corrected 2026-09-10:** concentrations *are* available, in Table 2 of the February 2024 and February 2026 reports (excluded from the compiled source markdown; read from the PDFs) — SW-East 18 µg/L, SW-West 15 µg/L, and BG-West, the **background** location, 17 µg/L, against a 2.0 µg/L MCL and a 15 µg/L reporting limit. Because the background location exceeded alongside the site locations, this is **not** a shift of a constituent into the surface-water pathway that the containment remedy's controls are meant to manage; it is a single anomalous sampling day at the detection floor. Thallium has been non-detect in every event since (February 2024, February 2025, February 2026), and DTSC approved each of those reports as final with no thallium comment. See [[thallium]].
