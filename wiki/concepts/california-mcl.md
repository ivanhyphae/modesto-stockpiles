California primary Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs) are the drinking-water thresholds against which the SR 132 Modesto Soil Stockpiles project has evaluated groundwater and stormwater sampling results at the site. In this case file, MCLs function as the decision line for whether a detected constituent is treated as a plain finding or as an exceedance requiring further attention in the record — most notably for barium and for thallium in stormwater, and for constituents such as chromium that are also compared against the California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSL) for soil.

## Role in the site's evaluation framework

The DEIR/EA uses the California primary MCL as the comparison standard for water-related results — groundwater monitoring and stormwater sampling — distinct from the California secondary MCL (used for aesthetic/non-health constituents) and from CHHSL (used for soil). Because the stockpiles sit on a site associated with the former FMC Corporation facility, the MCL comparison is the mechanism by which the project distinguishes constituents present at background or sub-threshold levels from those that constitute a reportable exceedance in water.

## Barium

Barium is the constituent in this file most directly tied to a stated primary MCL value and an exceedance history. Where the record reports barium results against the California primary MCL, exceedances are addressed in the [[barium]] and [[barium-mcl]] articles; the MCL is the threshold that determines whether a given round's barium detection is classed as compliant or exceeding.

## Other constituents compared to MCL

Thallium detections in stockpile soil (June 2014 Final FS Report) and in stormwater are evaluated against the primary MCL rather than CHHSL, since the comparison is to water quality; the December 2023 stormwater round is the only reported MCL exceedance for thallium at the site, following a March 2023 round that did not report one and followed by three rounds that did not either. Concentrations for these events *are* available, in Table 2 of the February 2024 and February 2026 reports — excluded from the compiled source markdown because data tables are dropped project-wide, so they must be read from the PDFs in `raw/`. Chromium results, by contrast, are compared in the record primarily against CHHSL for soil rather than MCL for water; see [[chromium]] for that comparison.

## Significance

The MCL is the operative threshold for any claim in this file that groundwater or stormwater is unimpacted: a round of sampling that stays below MCL for all reported constituents supports that claim, while a reported MCL exceedance undermines it for that constituent and that round — **unless the background location exceeds too, which is exactly what happened with thallium in December 2023** (background BG-West 17 µg/L against site locations at 18 and 15, with a 15 µg/L reporting limit versus a 2.0 µg/L MCL). An exceedance that appears equally at background is a statement about the sampling round or the method, not about the site. See [[thallium]]. The distinction between primary MCL (health-based, water) and CHHSL (health-based, soil) or secondary MCL (aesthetic) matters for liability review because a soil result exceeding CHHSL does not by itself mean a water result exceeds MCL, and the two should not be treated as interchangeable evidence of the same condition.
