Peak detections as a share of their regulatory benchmark

For each chemical, the highest verified detection in soil, groundwater and stormwater, divided by the benchmark that applies to that medium. Anything above the 100% line exceeded its benchmark at least once between 2004 and 2023.

Barium, lead and strontium are the site's designated Chemicals of Concern (COCs). Arsenic, manganese and nitrate are not — they are shown for context.

Designated Chemicals of Concern (COCs) are barium, lead and strontium. Arsenic, manganese and nitrate are not COCs — they are tracked as broader Chemicals of Potential Concern (COPCs) and secondary-standard parameters, and are shown here for context. An exceedance by one of those three is not a COC exceedance.

Arsenic's soil CHHSL of 0.07 mg/kg sits below the site's own offsite background (95% UCL 1.2 mg/kg), so every soil sample on earth exceeds it. That bar reflects the conservatism of the screening level, not a stockpile-specific arsenic problem.

Every maximum is drawn from analysis/detections-rebuild/combined_detections_summary.csv, restricted to verified observed maxima at non-background locations — consultant-derived statistics (exposure point concentration, 95% upper confidence limit, maximum detected concentration) are excluded, as are rows flagged unsupported. Percentages are computed by analysis/scripts/build_percent_of_benchmark.py, never asserted by hand. Manganese and nitrate were not analyzed in soil in the compiled reports.

Soil sampling events in the table: PSI — Preliminary Site Investigation (2004); SI — Site Investigation (2006); SSI — Supplemental Site Investigation (2012). Groundwater wells are labeled MW-n; stormwater sampling locations PL-n.
