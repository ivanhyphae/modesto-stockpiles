Designated Chemicals of Concern (COCs) are barium, lead and strontium. Arsenic, manganese and nitrate are not COCs — they are tracked as broader Chemicals of Potential Concern (COPCs) and secondary-standard parameters, and are shown here for context. An exceedance by one of those three is not a COC exceedance.

Arsenic's soil CHHSL of 0.07 mg/kg sits below the site's own offsite background (95% UCL 1.2 mg/kg), so every soil sample on earth exceeds it. That bar reflects the conservatism of the screening level, not a stockpile-specific arsenic problem.

Every maximum is drawn from analysis/detections-rebuild/combined_detections_summary.csv, restricted to verified observed maxima at non-background locations. Derived statistics (exposure point concentration, 95% upper confidence limit) are excluded, as are rows flagged unsupported. Soil values must additionally come from a primary per-sample table describing material that came out of the stockpiles: the reports that only restate earlier investigations in narrative are excluded, as is soil characterised as a candidate clean-fill source from elsewhere, and so is the SR 99 aerially deposited lead survey, which measures roadway-derived lead rather than stockpile contamination. Percentages are computed by analysis/scripts/build_percent_of_benchmark.py, never asserted by hand. Manganese and nitrate were not analyzed in soil in the compiled reports.

Every bar is now the highest value in its own set of dots — the peak and the distribution describe the same samples. Excluded from both: background stations, the confirmation resamples taken after contaminated soil had already been dug out, split duplicates, and the regulatory reference values that sit in the source tables in the same shape as results. Non-detects are counted but not plotted: a result of "less than 1.0" is not a measurement of 1.0, and drawing it as one would invent data at the reporting limit. Distributions are generated by analysis/scripts/build_sample_points.py and ship as a separate file, points.js.

Soil sampling events in the table: PSI — Preliminary Site Investigation (2004); SI — Site Investigation (2006); SSI — Supplemental Site Investigation (2012). Groundwater wells are labeled MW-n; stormwater sampling locations PL-n.
